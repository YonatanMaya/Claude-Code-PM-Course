# When a responder goes quiet — what we'd build instead

For Helen · from the Dispatch quiet-responder investigation · September 2026

---

## Who this is for

**Kip**, handling two responders — Meteor Mite and The Gale — and anyone
else running a roster where one card's gone dead quiet next to one
that's on fire. Kip's own words to Sofia earlier this month: *"Meteor
Mite's been texting me all week asking if something's broken because
it's so quiet... I'm looking at two cards on the same screen that
might as well be two different products."*

And **Meteor Mite** himself — a responder who used to take 10–12
callouts a week, dropped to 1 the week of 24 August, and hasn't heard
from us since. He filed nothing. Nobody did, on his behalf, until Kip
happened to mention it in an unrelated interview. He has no way today
to know whether he's still "in the system," and no way to do anything
about it if he's not.

This is written around those two, but it's the same story for Farlight
and Linda Pruitt, for The Undertow and Desmond Okafor, for Vesper and
Aunt Dot — everyone caught in the same trap since 12 August.

## What changes for them, once this exists

**For Meteor Mite:** right now, once his standing with the routing
system drops low enough, the only way it goes back up is by getting
offered a callout and taking it — and he's not getting offered
anything, so there's no way back in. Wen flagged this exact gap years
ago and nobody came back to it. What we'd build: his standing quietly
recovers on its own over the following weeks, whether he's getting
offered anything or not, the same way it would have eased off a slow
patch before 4.2 ever shipped. He doesn't do anything differently. He
doesn't need to know a mechanism changed. He just starts hearing from
us again, without having had to survive weeks of silence to earn that
back.

**For Kip:** he doesn't get a dial, a report, or a "responder health"
panel to check. What he notices is simpler and comes to him unasked —
Meteor Mite's card starts filling back in over the next couple of
weeks, the way it always used to when someone had a slow stretch. He
stops being the one explaining to a responder why the phone's gone
dead with nothing to point to. The gap between his two cards narrows
back toward the normal kind of lumpy, not the all-or-nothing split
he's been describing.

**For a responder who's genuinely just having a slow patch** — not
Meteor Mite's situation, just an ordinary quiet week — nothing changes
for them either, because nothing was broken for them in the first
place.

## What this deliberately doesn't do

- **It's not a setting.** Nobody at Rook is flipping a switch on a
  specific responder, and no handler gets a manual override. This is
  the system correcting itself, automatically, the same way for
  everyone — not a case-by-case fix applied to the four people we
  happened to find.
- **It doesn't touch the proximity change from 4.2.** That was a real,
  long-standing ask from responders working wide geographies, and nothing
  here reopens it. A responder farther from an incident still ranks
  behind someone closer. This fixes the trap, not the tradeoff.
- **It doesn't promise everyone equal volume.** Some responders will
  still get offered more than others — that's routing working as
  intended. What it removes is the specific failure where falling
  behind once became permanent, with no way back regardless of
  behavior.
- **It doesn't fix things instantly on deploy day.** Recovery happens
  gradually, over the following weeks — not a reset that erases
  August and hands everyone back their old numbers overnight.
- **It doesn't expose a visible "score" to anyone.** Not to handlers,
  not to responders. Surfacing that number is a much bigger product
  decision than this one, and isn't part of what's being proposed here.
