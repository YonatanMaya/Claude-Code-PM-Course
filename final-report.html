Release 4.2 quietly locked four responders out of Rook Dispatch. Here's the way back.

Six sessions with one folder of company documents, two piles of feedback, a data file and a codebase. I didn't read code, and every question was in plain English. This is what I asked, what I found, and what I'd build.

Rook Industries is a fictional teaching scenario. Every number here traces back to a file in 00-rook/.

The answer, before the evidence

Four people stopped being asked. Nothing in Dispatch could let them back in.

What happened

Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow and Vesper went from about 12 pings a week to 0–1. They weren't turning work down: they accepted 69–82% of offers before 4.2.

Why

A missed ping counts as a "no". 4.2 cut the window from 90 to 60 seconds, so more yeses became misses. A low standing only recovers when you're offered something, and a low standing is what stops the offers.

What I'd build

A way back: standing that recovers on its own each week, a missed ping that costs less than a no, 90 seconds again, and a floor. And this week, talk to the four directly.

Module 4 · X-Ray Vision · Inspect codebases without coding

The bug isn't in what 4.2 changed. It's in what the code never did.

The technique: ask what it does, in plain English with the file for each step. Then ask what it doesn't do.

What it does

  1. Find who's free in the region, and how far away they areavailability.py
  2. Score everyone: closeness 0.60 · yes-rate 0.25 · skills 0.15routing.py
  3. Ping the top person and wait 60 secondsoffer.py
  4. A no or a miss: move to the next personoffer.py
  5. A yes adds +0.08; a no or a miss takes −0.12history.py

What it doesn't do

  • Tell a miss from a no. "Everything downstream treats the last two the same way."
  • Let a score recover on its own. Wen asked in 2019: "should this ease back toward NEUTRAL_SCORE on its own after a while?" Nobody came back to it.
  • Reach the bottom of a busy list. Everyone is on it, but it stops at the first yes.
  • Recover fairly. One miss takes one and a half accepted jobs to undo.
  • Keep a record. Scores live in memory and no rank is logged.

Marcus's question from 14 Aug, answered

"was that meant to apply to responders who've been turning jobs down too, or only everyone else? … a decision or if it just fell out that way" → It fell out that way. There's one formula, and it can't tell someone slow to reach their phone from someone turning work down.

My promptFind me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
Module 5 · Super-Speed · Turn descriptions into prototypes

Not a setting. Something Kip would notice, and Meteor Mite would feel.

The technique: describe it the way you would at a whiteboard, then ask for a rough version you can click.

Who it's for

Meteor Mite: from 10–12 pings a week to 1, and no way to know if he's still in the system. Kip, who watched him go quiet while The Gale, also his, got 21 pings the same week.

What changes for them

Standing drifts back toward normal every week. A miss costs −0.04 instead of −0.12. Pings wait 90 seconds again. Nobody sits below a floor of 0.20. Kip's card fills back in on its own.

What it deliberately doesn't do

No setting, no manual override, no visible score. The proximity change stays. Nobody with a good record gets pulled down. Recovery takes weeks, not overnight.

How we'll know it worked

  • Within 4 weeks, each of the four is back to at least 6 pings a week
  • No available responder under 2 a week, two weeks running, while a regionmate is above baseline
  • Pool acceptance stays at or above 75–78%

Measured in the export, not in tickets: slide 3 shows why.

What I built, and what it can't prove

prototype.html: Kip's real coverage screen and Meteor Mite's phone, before and after, week by week.

routing-fix-simulator.html: the real scoring rules, 4.2 vs. 4.3, across sixteen responders. It reproduces the trap, but it doesn't show the fix getting them out: it's too small (1–3 pings a week against 11 in the real data). Hence the decision rule: if the four aren't back to 6 within four weeks, add a minimum weekly share of pings.

Credibility · what I don't know, and where I was wrong

Three things I'm confident about. Four I'm not. Two I got wrong along the way.

confident

  • The four collapsed in one week, starting the week after release. Two independent calculations agree.
  • It wasn't them: normal acceptance before, different handlers, and the same handler got one of each.
  • The code has no way back for a low score. It's in the file, and Wen flagged it in 2019.

not yet known

  • Whether the mechanism holds in production logs, not just the code and the weekly export
  • Why 17 tickets report silence in weeks the export shows normal volume
  • Whether the four are simply farther away, in which case the fix alone isn't enough
  • Whether last August was also soft, since the data starts in June

corrected

  • I first counted the silence tickets as evidence. Checked row by row, only 4 of 21 match.
  • I said the simulator showed the fix working. Re-run headlessly, it doesn't, because it's too small to tell.

Both corrections are in CLAUDE.md, so the next session starts from what's true.
